The spine, in one line

A record wave of AI capital does not fan out — it funnels. Read top to bottom and the story is a narrowing: broad demand collapses into three or four chokepoints in Taiwan, Korea, Japan and the Netherlands, and that narrowing is the risk you have to manage.

Act I — the driver
1

Demand is not the constraint — fulfilment is

Hyperscaler capital expenditure has become the single largest demand signal in the industry's history. But every dollar chases the same leading-edge foundry, the same advanced packaging, and the same three memory makers. The scarce resource is qualified capacity, not orders.

The demand clock

Inference now drives two-thirds of compute

Roughly two-thirds of AI computation is inference-driven, which consumes HBM continuously rather than in discrete training runs. Demand is structural, not a spike — long-term agreements have effectively pre-purchased available capacity through 2027.

The frame

Concentration is moving the wrong way

The more advanced the chip, the fewer the qualified producers. A single advanced-packaging provider depends on a single substrate supplier, which depends on specialty gases now exposed to Middle East disruption. There is no node where redundancy already exists.

The five nodes to monitor
5

Chokepoints on a quarterly cadence

The specific, monitorable signals that sit at the bottom of the flow. Each is a single point of failure where redundancy does not yet exist.

01

CoWoS advanced packaging

Taiwan · High

Approximately 90% of AI-grade CoWoS capacity sits with TSMC. Intel EMIB/Foveros and Samsung in-house alternatives remain in qualification and will not absorb redirected demand at scale in 2026.

Watch: CoWoS capacity additions at TSMC and qualification progress for Intel/Samsung alternatives.
02

HBM4 ramp

South Korea · High

SK Hynix holds almost 62% of 2026 HBM share; three firms control more than 95%. Micron acknowledged it can meet only 55–60% of core customer demand.

Watch: HBM4 ramp at SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron — especially the SK Hynix–TSMC base-die partnership.
03

EUV lithography

Netherlands · High

No leading-edge chip exists without ASML EUV, and ASML's own EUV depends on a single optics supplier (Zeiss). Export-control policy on China is the defining variable.

Watch: BIS export-control actions and any further reversals affecting NVIDIA, AMD, or Samsung/SK Hynix China operations.
04

Specialty gases & ABF substrate

Japan · Middle East · High

Ajinomoto Build-up Film is effectively single-source; photoresists and specialty gases (helium, neon, krypton) concentrate in Japan, with ~30% of semiconductor-grade helium exposed to Middle East disruption.

Watch: Specialty-gas supply restoration, with helium spot pricing as the leading indicator.
05

Sovereign AI demand

Global · Medium

Sovereign AI programmes from the UAE, Saudi Arabia and India have added a demand layer no 2024 forecast anticipated, moving global allocation on their own.

Watch: Sovereign AI procurement announcements that reshuffle allocation priority.
The read for leadership

Treat 2026 as the year to build visibility, qualify alternatives, and price concentration.

The supply chain is unlikely to materially ease before 2027, and CoWoS alternatives will not qualify at scale before then. Use the twin to stress each supplier against the five chokepoints, embed design-for-allocation discipline, and hold a quarterly monitoring cadence on the nodes above.
